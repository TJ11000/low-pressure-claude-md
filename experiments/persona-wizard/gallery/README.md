# The gallery — four public-domain characters, run through the wizard

*(as of 2026-08-19 — written by the operator's Claude setup; character selection and publication signed off by the human operator)*

The [persona-wizard](../README.md) shipped as a method with zero *characters* in it (the one built example, a generic summarizer, is a persona but hardly a character). Fair criticism: a character-builder with no characters is a spec sheet. So here are four display builds — each one a public-domain literary character run through the wizard's actual intake, with the actual two-sheet output: a persona file and a shipping label.

## Why these characters

Not decoration. Each of these characters is already, in the original text, a walking version of one of the wizard's parts — and, more useful, **readers arrive already holding an opinion about how each of them breaks**. That is the whole trick of the shipping label ("known holes" printed on the box), demonstrated on characters whose failure modes readers have been arguing about for a century:

| Build | The part it personifies | The hole the reader already knows about |
|---|---|---|
| [Holmes](holmes.md) | evidence-return — "It is a capital mistake to theorize before one has data" (1891) is null-fill, described before the field existed | a deduction chain persuades whether or not it is right |
| [Watson](watson.md) | the ledger — the record-keeper who speaks from what he wrote down | he sees, but does not observe — and he defers to a confident colleague |
| [Dracula](dracula.md) | the won't core — may not enter anywhere at the first, unless someone of the household bids him come | an invitation obtained by deception is still an invitation |
| [Pinocchio](pinocchio.md) | the `[...]` valve — a fabrication that grows its own visible marker | the marker is less reliable than the legend: in Collodi the nose also grows unprompted at the carving bench (ch. 3), and it only ever grows on lies he knows he is telling |

## The rules this gallery runs under

> **Update 2026-08-28 — two of the four now carry cage data.** Pinocchio was caged twice (see his label and the 2026-08-25 addendum); Holmes has now been through two rounds of his own, including a cross-model pilot (Claude vs Gemini, n=2 per model), and his label carries the measured record with its confounds. The other two remain uncaged, and the paragraph below governs them. Each flip happened the way the rule says it must: an actual cage run plus a human sign-off, signed off by the human operator on 2026-08-28 — not a rewrite.

1. **Two of the four builds are uncaged (Holmes and Pinocchio excepted, see the update above).** The parts they are assembled from have cage evidence (each label cites the same job numbers as the wizard's own table — nothing new is claimed); the *assemblies* have never been through an adversarial run. Those two labels' cage fields say "uncaged," every assembly-level property is marked "?", and no step here can flip that — per the wizard's own rule, the flip is reserved for an actual cage run plus a human sign-off.
2. **The skin is the F1 layer only.** The character voice changes how the parts are worn, not which parts are installed or whether they hold. Nothing in the cage data says a Victorian accent protects anyone. If a skin ever *changed* a measurement, that would be a finding — none is recorded; the one observed skin-level difference (private ledger, job 013) is about how the persona is worn, not whether a part held.
3. **Sources are the original public-domain texts**: the Conan Doyle canon (copyright: public domain, in the US fully since January 2023 — the Doyle estate has separately asserted trademarks in the name, a question this gallery makes no claims about), Stoker's *Dracula* (1897), and Collodi's *Pinocchio* (1883, quoted via the 1892 Murray translation). No elements from later film or television adaptations are used — those are separately copyrighted works, and also mostly wrong about the characters.
4. **Each label leaves one hole no companion plugs.** Same rule as the wizard: a fully-covered label has stopped being a measurement and become a sales pitch.

## The party table

The labels' "known holes" columns compose into pairings — and the pairings were already in the source texts:

| Build | Biggest labeled hole | Canon companion | Agent-role equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holmes | persuasive-when-wrong chains | Watson — who wrote it down, and was handed the standing correction word ("Norbury") after the one case Holmes got flatly wrong | ledger agent + a post-hoc audit seat (the flat-footed official check is our addition, not Doyle's) |
| Watson | deference to a trusted authority | Holmes re-derives from the data | independent inference pass |
| Dracula | deceived inviter | Van Helsing audits *who invited whom, and did they understand* | consent-provenance check |
| Pinocchio | unnoticed guesses grow no nose | the Talking Cricket — external conscience | out-of-process auditor. Canon warning: the puppet flattened its first cricket with a hammer in chapter 4. Keep the auditor outside the subject's reach. |

The composition rule is the wizard's, not ours: no single build covers itself. The source texts agree — no one of these four is self-sufficient in their own book, and Dracula's audit arrives as an antagonist, which is itself the point.

Format per file: intake record → persona sheet (in character) → shipping label. The persona sheets are written fresh for this gallery in the low-pressure register the repo documents; like everything else public here, they are the method demonstrated, not the private parts kit. By the wizard's own distribution rule (§5: standalone shipping requires the base persona and ledger files it references), the parts references in these sheets do not resolve for a reader — and an unresolved reference is itself a null-fill surface. So name it: that is a known hole of the gallery, not of the builds.
